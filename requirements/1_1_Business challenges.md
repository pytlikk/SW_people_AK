# Business Challenges & Pain Points

## Business Challenges

- **No popularity signal**: The estate has no real idea which rides, zones, or animal displays attract guests, so capital investment and staff deployment are difficult to justify or time correctly.
- **Growth-or-sell pressure**: Visitation must triple (5,000 → 15,000 daily) within three years, or the family may be forced to sell the carnivorous plant collection. Growth must also increase profit, not just headcount through the gate.
- **Animal-care cost curve**: Looking after 200+ exotic and poisonous animals is expensive in the healthy case and sharply worse when they get sick. The jumping-piranha collection additionally needs population control, not only individual health notes.
- **Loyalty gap**: The Countess wants more returning visitors and does not know which offers, experiences, or prices would produce them.
- **Disconnected operations on a sprawling site**: Patchy Wi-Fi, historic rides, and a newly public hazardous animal collection must be operated as one estate, not as isolated clipboards.

## Pain Points of the Business

- Ticket sales (including family passes) are a must-have, but there is no modern access, entitlement, or yield system behind them.
- Staff are deployed by habit and anecdote; queues form at unknown bottlenecks while other areas sit empty.
- Ride popularity, downtime, and maintenance cost are not joined, so a failed popular ride’s lost yield is invisible.
- Animal health, feeding quality (“how much / how well they are eating”), and piranha population are not tracked in a way that supports early intervention.
- First-time guests leave without a reason or a channel to come back; there is no membership or experiment loop.
- Field systems cannot assume always-on connectivity; anything that requires the cloud in the checkout or gate hot path will fail on a normal park day.
- Opening a previously private poisonous/exotic collection to the public creates liability that paper logs will not defend.
